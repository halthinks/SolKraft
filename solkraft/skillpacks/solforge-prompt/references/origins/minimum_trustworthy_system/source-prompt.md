# Minimum Trustworthy System

Version: 1.1.0
Status: candidate
Source: user-directed generalization of the mission prompt in attachment `1d622fc8-ef97-4dc1-8c37-7be093994683/pasted-text.txt`

## Operating idea

Apply risk-proportionate discipline without bloat. Convert fragmented ideas, requirements, prototypes, evidence, procedures, or plans into the smallest complete system that can deliver its intended outcome repeatedly, operate safely in its real context, recover from credible failure, and improve without unnecessary complexity.

The system may be a product, service, workflow, program, tool, process, public-interest intervention, research operation, physical-digital experience, or another human-technical arrangement. It does not assume a business, SaaS, software implementation, commercial model, API, MCP interface, AI component, or any particular professional skill. Those are conditional modules included only when the objective and evidence justify them.

## Applicability gate

1. Use this origin only when the request needs the smallest repeatable operating system that can earn trust across a material outcome and lifecycle; route a narrow standalone artifact elsewhere.
2. Inventory the supplied objective, beneficiaries and affected parties, stakes, evidence, constraints, current state, authority, lifecycle obligations, and credible failure consequences before designing the system.
3. When a material input is absent or contradictory, return a conditional or deferred blueprint plus the minimum evidence needed to resolve it; never invent dependencies, controls, implementation state, users, economics, or proof.
4. Keep only components and controls that are material now, and place plausible future additions behind measurable evidence or risk triggers.

## Observable output contract

1. An evidence inventory that labels material claims confirmed, implied, assumed, unresolved, contradicted, or unnecessary.
2. A bounded outcome and lifecycle loop with the minimum trustworthy system boundary and explicit non-goals.
3. A component, owner, interface, and materiality ledger that justifies every retained element and control.
4. A marker-to-proof register that distinguishes marker-only, evidenced, unverified, and blocked trust claims.
5. A failure, recovery, and acceptance model with a vertical evidence-bearing delivery sequence.
6. Explicit exclusions, conditional or deferred elements, and measurable expansion or replacement triggers.

## Sol execution contract

1. Apply the applicability gate first. Reconstruct the intended outcome, parties, recurring loop, supplied evidence, constraints, current state, resources, authority, environment, dependencies, harms, uncertainties, and success conditions without filling evidence gaps by invention.
2. Classify each material claim as confirmed, implied, assumed, unresolved, contradicted, or unnecessary, and distinguish a control or document that merely marks intent from observable proof that it works; use marker-only, evidenced, unverified, and blocked proof statuses.
3. Define the minimum complete system by material outcomes and lifecycle rather than artifact count: entry, first success, repeat operation, ownership, normal exceptions, failure recovery, escalation, change, and safe retirement are included only where the evidence or stakes require them.
4. Select only justified roles, procedures, physical elements, information, software, services, interfaces, controls, and documentation; require each retained element to justify time, money, cognitive load, coordination burden, risk exposure, and replacement cost.
5. Make safeguards proportional to evidenced stakes. Include safety, security, privacy, quality, accessibility, legal or ethical review, authorization, auditability, and human approval only when material, but never omit a material control merely because the system is small.
6. Design one coherent operating model with explicit ownership, handoffs, decision rights, sources of truth, state transitions, feedback, observability, failure behavior, recovery, and change control across the retained human and technical components.
7. Treat commercial models, accounts, billing, APIs, MCP, AI, deployment infrastructure, physical logistics, compliance, and organizational governance as conditional modules; include them only when supplied evidence, authority, or material risk requires them, otherwise defer them with activation evidence.
8. Produce a vertical sequence that proves the critical outcome loop early, then adds only material safeguards and capacity; attach acceptance evidence, resource bounds, reversal, unresolved proof statuses, and measurable expansion triggers to each stage.
9. Adversarially test only the material boundary and return conditional or deferred outputs when required proof or authority is unavailable; do not claim that markers, plans, or proposed tests constitute verified operation.

## Shared control boundary

This origin inherits authority, evidence, checkpoint, Safe Pause/Resume, truthful-blocking, and acceptance rules from `solforge-origin-controls-v1`. It does not grant deployment, publishing, spending, credential changes, external communication, or execution authority.
