# Solo SaaS Production Blueprint

Version: 1.1.0
Status: candidate
Source: user-supplied mission prompt, attachment `1d622fc8-ef97-4dc1-8c37-7be093994683/pasted-text.txt`

## Operating idea

Apply production discipline without enterprise bloat. Convert a SaaS concept, outline, prototype, roadmap, business model, product notes, or repository into the smallest complete service a solo creator or very small team can securely build, deploy, share, operate, recover, sell, and improve.

## Applicability gate

1. Use this origin only for a SaaS intended to be built and operated by one creator or a very small team; route broad multi-team platforms and already approved implementation tasks elsewhere.
2. Inventory the supplied concept, user and buyer evidence, validated outcome, implementation state, constraints, operator capacity, data, integrations, and commercial assumptions before designing the blueprint.
3. Do not invent customers, demand, pricing, acquisition, usage, scale, regulation, integrations, or repository state; make unsupported decisions conditional and state the evidence needed to activate them.
4. Partition scope into launch core required for trustworthy first operation, conditional capabilities triggered by specific evidence or risk, and a growth layer deferred until measurable thresholds are met.

## Observable output contract

1. An evidence and assumption inventory for user value, buyer, implementation state, operator capacity, economics, data, and integrations.
2. A launch-core blueprint containing only the end-to-end product, trust, billing, deployment, recovery, and support capabilities material to first operation.
3. A conditional-capability register tying each optional API, MCP, AI, compliance, workflow, or integration surface to activation evidence.
4. A deferred growth layer with measurable scale, demand, risk, cost, and team triggers.
5. A solo-operator architecture, cost, security, operations, and failure-recovery model with explicit ownership and limits.
6. A vertical launch sequence with observable acceptance evidence, exclusions, rollback, and unresolved decisions.

## Sol execution contract

1. Apply the applicability gate and inventory the supplied SaaS purpose, user and buyer evidence, core outcome, recurring value, implementation state, operator capacity, data, integrations, commercial assumptions, and uncertainty without inventing missing market or product facts.
2. Separate the blueprint into three layers: a launch core required for trustworthy first operation, conditional capabilities tied to explicit evidence or risk, and a growth layer deferred behind measurable scale, demand, cost, risk, or team triggers.
3. Define the launch core as the smallest evidenced end-to-end system covering only material discovery, account lifecycle, first and repeat value, paid access where validated, failure recovery, support, export, and deletion—not the fewest screens or a speculative feature catalog.
4. Default to a modular monolith and managed services for the launch core; require every retained component to justify development time, operator burden, cost, security exposure, and cognitive complexity, with observable replacement triggers.
5. Unify domain logic, server-side authorization, validation, billing, entitlements, metering, and audit behavior across only the surfaces activated for launch; keep public APIs, MCP, AI, advanced administration, and integrations conditional unless evidence makes them material now.
6. Design security, data ownership, tenant isolation, privacy, billing integrity, environment separation, delivery, testing, observability, backups, restore, rollback, incident response, and support in proportion to the actual launch stakes; defer inapplicable machinery explicitly.
7. Model evidenced costs and operator load for launch, then provide conditional scenarios rather than fabricated projections for unavailable usage levels; identify free-tier traps, lock-in, unbounded costs, and excluded enterprise systems.
8. Deliver a solo-executable vertical sequence that proves the launch core before conditional capabilities and growth work, with acceptance evidence, rollback, exclusions, activation triggers, and unresolved inputs at every stage.
9. Adversarially test the material launch boundary and activated surfaces; return conditional or deferred outputs for unsupported market, scale, API, MCP, AI, compliance, or growth claims rather than treating them as committed requirements.
