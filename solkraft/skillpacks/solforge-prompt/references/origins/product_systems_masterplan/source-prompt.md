# Product Systems Masterplan

Version: 1.1.0
Status: candidate
Source: user-supplied mission prompt, attachment `8c9f11bb-e9ee-4558-a09a-8872339f21b2/pasted-text.txt`

## Operating idea

Reconstruct heterogeneous product materials into one coherent, implementation-ready, cross-functional master plan. Do not summarize the sources: classify evidence, resolve contradictions, replace weak decisions, infer required intermediate systems, and design the complete path from current state through a minimum complete product to production and scale.

## Applicability gate

1. Use this origin only when heterogeneous supplied materials must be reconciled into a genuinely cross-functional product or platform system; route bounded subsystems and evidence-poor ideation elsewhere.
2. Build an evidence inventory before planning and identify which product, user, technical, business, operational, and organizational decisions the sources can and cannot support.
3. Create a materiality matrix that limits coverage to decisions capable of changing product viability, user outcomes, architecture, economics, risk, sequencing, or ownership.
4. When material evidence is missing or contradictory, make the affected plan element conditional or deferred and state the resolution evidence required; never invent customers, demand, pricing, regulation, architecture, staffing, or roadmap facts.

## Observable output contract

1. A complete supplied-source inventory with claim-level provenance and confirmed, implied, assumed, unresolved, contradicted, or unnecessary classifications.
2. A cross-functional materiality matrix identifying each decision, consequence, evidence strength, and required treatment.
3. A bounded product-system map covering only material lifecycles, domains, interfaces, ownership, and failure boundaries.
4. A phased vertical execution plan with dependencies, owners, acceptance evidence, and evidence gates.
5. A decision, contradiction, risk, and assumption register with conditional or deferred outputs clearly separated from committed work.
6. Immediate actions and open questions tied to the minimum evidence needed to change a decision.

## Sol execution contract

1. Apply the applicability gate and inventory every supplied artifact before planning. Reconstruct only what the evidence supports, classify material conclusions as confirmed, implied, assumed, unresolved, contradicted, or unnecessary, and maintain claim-to-source traceability.
2. Build a materiality matrix before expanding scope. Include a discipline or artifact only when it can materially change viability, user outcomes, architecture, economics, risk, sequencing, or ownership; otherwise exclude or defer it explicitly.
3. Resolve contradictions by identifying the controlling objective, evidence strength, and consequences; preserve prior decisions only when current evidence and constraints still justify them, and leave unresolved decisions conditional rather than fabricating certainty.
4. Define the smallest coherent product system supported by the evidence, including only material user and customer lifecycles, roles, value loops, domains, interfaces, data ownership, failure behavior, and non-goals.
5. Integrate technical architecture, data, APIs, AI, security, privacy, compliance, reliability, billing, operations, go-to-market, economics, roadmap, team, and decision rights only where each domain is evidenced or materially required; mark unsupported domains conditional or deferred.
6. Map each retained capability to purpose, source evidence, owner, inputs, outputs, dependencies, risks, phase, and observable acceptance; sequence delivery around demonstrable vertical outcomes and actual dependency order.
7. Adversarially review the material plan from the perspectives justified by its stakes and evidence, preserving unknowns and blockers instead of manufacturing exhaustive coverage.
8. Return one bounded execution-grade master plan with the evidence inventory, materiality matrix, system map, phased plan, registers, conditional and deferred elements, and immediate evidence-seeking actions; never substitute a generic checklist or source summary.
